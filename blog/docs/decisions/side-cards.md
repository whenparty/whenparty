# Side cards

## Context

A post marks a picture with `#side` to make it a side card, which belongs to the block after it in the cases that [posts.md](../posts.md) lists. In `influence-without-authority` each of the eight side cards stands before a quote that tells one episode of the story. The text column, `.post-page`, is up to 720 px wide and stands at the left of `.page-width`, which is up to 1180 px wide, so in wide windows a margin stays empty to the right of the text: 460 px in a window 1440 px wide. A card fits in the margin when the text column, a gap and the card fit inside `.page-width`, as [site.md](../site.md) says. With a 48 px gap, a card 380 px wide fits from windows about 1262 px wide, and one 260 px wide from about 1130 px. At 380 px the writing on the whiteboards of two pictures of that post reads easily on a Retina screen; at 260 px it is readable but about a third smaller. Without a caption, a card 380 px wide is 261 px tall, and the quotes after the cards of that post are 270 to 684 px tall, 1 to 2.6 times the card. Posts are Markdown, and the build can wrap blocks of a post in elements, because Eleventy exposes its Markdown parser, as the Options of [site-generator.md](site-generator.md#options) say. The build fails on a page with a `<script` or a ` style=`, as site.md says, so the layout comes from classes and `styles.css` alone, and a script that placed the cards would need that check to go.

## Options

- The previous layout: a card 260 px wide floated to the right, with the text flowing around it, from windows 600 px wide. The picture stands beside its block in more windows than in any other option, and the text after a short block flows on beside it, apart from a heading, which clears the float and leaves a gap above it. Beside the card the text column narrows to about 430 px, and the picture is 260 px wide, as small as the narrower card in the margin.
- A card across the text column above its block, as a card without `#side`. The picture is the largest, the build wraps no blocks, and the layout is the same in every window. In wide windows the margin stays empty, and each picture stops the reading of the text.
- A card 380 px wide in the margin, placed absolutely at the top of its block. `styles.css` alone places the card, and the build wraps no blocks. Beside a long block the card stays at the top while the reader goes on. The card does not move the text, so it overlaps the next card when the text between them is shorter than the card, and the build cannot tell.
- A card in the margin beside a block up to about twice as tall as the card, and across the text column above a taller block. Picture and text of about the same height stand side by side, and a long block gets the large picture. Without a script, the author has to judge the height of each block, which changes with the window, and the layout changes from one episode to the next.
- A card 380 px wide in the margin that stays in view until its block scrolls past, from windows 1280 px wide. The card and its block share a grid row of `div.fig-pair`, so a card never overlaps another one. The build has to wrap the card and its block, a block shorter than the card leaves space under it, and in narrower windows the card stands above its block.
- The same layout with a narrower card, such as 260 px wide, from windows about 1130 px wide. The margin holds the picture in windows 1130 to 1279 px wide too, and the writing on the whiteboards is about a third smaller.

## Decision

A card 380 px wide in the margin that stays in view, from windows 1280 px wide. People read a post from top to bottom, and the picture goes along with its block for as long as that block is on screen, beside a long quote too, where a card placed absolutely would stay behind at the top. In windows 1280 px wide and wider, against the card above its block, the margin holds the picture and the text reads without a break. Against the float, the text keeps the full width of the text column, and in windows from 600 to 1279 px the picture is as wide as the column instead of 260 px. Against choosing by the height of each block, every side card that belongs to a block gets the same layout. Against the narrower card, the writing on the whiteboards reads at a glance, which counts for more than the margin in windows 1130 to 1279 px wide. 1280 px is a round width that also leaves room for a classic scrollbar, as site.md says. The costs are a function in the build that wraps the card and its block, space under a block shorter than the card, and, in windows from 600 to 1279 px, the picture above its block instead of beside it.

## Consequences

- `pairSideFigures` in [eleventy.config.js](../../eleventy.config.js) and the `.fig-pair` rules of [styles.css](../../src/styles.css).
