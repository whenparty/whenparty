# Presentation

- `4-1` In the lobby of the 3D interface a player chooses their athlete's appearance: male or female body type, a hairstyle and the colors of its hair, skin, shirt, shorts and shoes. The body type defaults to male. Both body types have the same hairstyle and color choices. Changing the body type keeps those choices. The hairstyles are short, curly, bun and bald. A color is `#` followed by six hexadecimal digits in either case. Any other body type, hairstyle or color is rejected. Each choice the player has not made keeps the default appearance. The color palette and its defaults belong to the interface design. Choosing an appearance keeps the athlete's name, and entering a name keeps its appearance. Appearance changes are accepted only in the lobby, every player sees them, and they change no level, energy or score.
- `4-2` The 3D interface is the default page, and the text interface is at `text.html`. Players of both interfaces can be in one room, and a player continues their athlete in either one. Invite links open the default page. The preparation, scoring and match requirements apply to both interfaces. The scenes, movements and appearance controls in this specification apply to the 3D interface.
- `4-3` During preparation the 3D interface shows the player's athlete. Its arms are larger at each arms level than at the level below, from 0 to 10, and its legs likewise follow its legs level. Other characteristics do not change how it looks. Plans are not animated.
- `4-4` Every challenge's announcement repeats an ideal demonstration of that challenge's movement (4-5). The results show all athletes in the common room. During the shared queue ([3-5](3-match.md)), the camera focuses on the current athlete and only that athlete performs; the others wait. The attempt follows 4-9 and finishes by the tenth second of the turn. Full completion is followed by celebration, partial completion by fatigue, and not performed by disappointment. The reaction fills the rest of the turn. Both body types use these movements and reactions at every arms and legs level. Revealed results stay visible while later athletes perform. After the queue ends, a player can replay the performances on their own page; replaying changes nothing in the championship or on other players' pages.
- `4-5` The ideal demonstrations and full attempts use these movements and apparatus:

  | Challenge | Apparatus | Movement |
  | - | - | - |
  | Push-ups | Floor mat | Lower a straight body by bending the elbows, then push back up. |
  | Squats | Floor mat | Bend the knees and hips into a squat, then stand with both feet planted. |
  | Pull-ups | Horizontal bar | Pull up from a hang until the chin clears the bar, then lower back to the hang. |
  | L-sit | Low parallel handles | Support the body on straight arms and hold both straight legs together in front, above the floor. |
  | Pistol squat | Floor mat | Squat on one planted foot with the other leg extended forward, then stand on the same foot. |
  | Handstand | Floor mat | Hold the body upright over the hands with straight arms and legs, then return the feet to the floor. |
  | Muscle-up | Horizontal bar | Pull up from a hang, bring the chest over the bar and press into support above it, then return to the hang. |
  | Burpees | Floor mat | Move from standing through a squat into a plank, perform a push-up, bring the feet forward and finish with a small jump. |
  | Front lever | Horizontal bar | Raise the straight body from a hang into a horizontal hold below the bar, then lower back to the hang. |
  | Human flag | Vertical wall bars | Grip two rungs at different heights and hold the straight body horizontally out to the side, then lower the feet to the floor. |
- `4-6` After the tenth challenge's queue ends, the results stand the athletes on a podium. The athletes with the highest championship points stand on the first step, those with the next lower total on the second, and those with the total after that on the third. Athletes with equal points share a step, and a step without athletes stays empty. Every other athlete stands beside the podium. Every athlete's name and championship points stay visible. With totals 1000, 1000, 800, 600 and 400, two athletes stand on the first step, one on each of the other steps and one beside the podium.
- `4-7` If the 3D interface cannot show its scene, because the browser has no WebGL or the scene fails to load, the page says so and links to the text interface for the same room. When the browser asks for reduced motion (`prefers-reduced-motion`), the 3D interface shows still poses instead of animations.
- `4-8` The apparatus appears with the demonstration or current performance; waiting athletes do not each get their own apparatus. The preparation scene has no challenge apparatus. The camera keeps the whole performing athlete and the used part of the apparatus visible. Hands stay at their grips during supported movements, and planted hands and feet stay on their supports. The body does not pass through the floor or apparatus. Attempts finish with a controlled return or stop; an unsuccessful attempt does not turn into a somersault. Apparatus stays visible with the still poses for reduced motion.
- `4-9` Push-ups, squats, pull-ups, pistol squats, muscle-ups and burpees use a set of five repetitions. A full attempt completes all five, with each repetition visibly returning to its starting position. L-sit, handstand, front lever and human flag use one hold: a full attempt enters the pose, holds it steadily for eight seconds and returns to its support. If only stamina is below the challenge's requirement, the athlete completes fewer repetitions or a shorter steady hold, then stops from fatigue. Its completed repetitions are 1 + floor(4 · stamina / required stamina); its steady hold lasts the same fraction of eight seconds as those repetitions are of five. Thus stamina 0 allows one repetition or a 1.6-second hold, and stamina 1 when 2 is required allows three repetitions or a 4.8-second hold. If any other required characteristic is below its requirement, the first attempt is incomplete, with no complete repetition or steady hold. A score of zero likewise shows an unsuccessful start. These movements use the levels and shortfall already used for scoring ([2-1 and 2-2](2-scoring.md)); their repetition counts and hold times do not change championship points. The 3D interface shows completed repetitions out of five, or elapsed steady-hold time out of eight seconds. Reduced motion uses a representative still pose and shows the final achieved repetition count or hold duration without animating the counter.
